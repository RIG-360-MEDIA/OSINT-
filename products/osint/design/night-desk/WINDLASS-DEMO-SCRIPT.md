# Windlass Company OSINT — Live Demo Script

A page-by-page walkthrough script for presenting the product to Windlass Steelcrafts.
Everything on screen is real, sourced data (July 2026). Where a figure is a live
government feed in production, say so plainly — it builds trust, it doesn't cost you.

**Presenter notes:** log in at desk.rig360media.com → pick **Windlass — Company OSINT**.
It opens in dark ("Gunmetal") theme. The ☾/☀ button top-right flips to light if the
room prefers it. Left rail is your page-by-page map. Speak to the *decision* each page
supports, not the chart.

> "You already make the best military and ceremonial swords in the world — that's not in
> question. What you *don't* have is a single place that tells you where the next contract
> is, which rival is circling it, and where you're quietly losing ground. That's what this
> is. It pulls tenders, competitors, trade flows, defence budgets and social signal into one
> intelligence desk, and every screen ends with a recommended move — not just a number.
> Let me walk you through it."

Then log in and choose the Windlass product.

## 1 · Command Overview — "the one-screen read"

> "This is the morning screen — everything that matters in ten seconds. Four numbers."

**Point at the four KPI tiles:**
- **Government opportunities on watch — 3.** "One you already hold, two to pursue. We're
  honest about it: none is a tender open to bid *this morning* — that's why it says 'on
  watch'. In the live product the portals feed this automatically."
- **Reachable ceremonial armies — 56.** "The 56 Commonwealth nations all run British-pattern
  drill and dress swords — the exact patterns you already make — plus all 32 NATO members.
  That's the size of your real buyable universe."
- **Rivals who can win a national contract — 2.** "Only Pooley Sword and WKC of Solingen can
  actually bid a whole-of-forces dress-sword contract. Everyone else fights over collectors.
  Your competitive world is smaller than it feels."
- **Social reach gap vs Cold Steel — 145×.** "Here's the uncomfortable one. A budget knife
  brand with zero government contracts has 145 times your Instagram reach. We'll come back
  to that — it's the most fixable weakness in the whole picture."

**Point at "Where to focus next":** "Your markets, ranked. UK 92, US 84, India 80, Saudi 78 —
budget, tradition and how easily you can reach them, in one score."

**Point at "Top moves this week":** "And three moves, in plain English: defend the UK contract
you already hold, open the Gulf through an agent, and fix the online invisibility. Every page
in this tool rolls up to moves like these."

## 2 · Tender Intelligence — the priority

> "This is the heart of it. Where the money is."

**Point at the honesty banner first:** "Straight away — as of today we can't confirm a single
public sword tender that's open to bid. That's the truth of this market: it doesn't run on open
portals, it runs on standing supply and relationships. In production this line goes green the
moment a real solicitation appears on SAM.gov, UK Find-a-Tender, GeM, TED or the UN marketplace."

**Walk the four columns:**
- **Held — defend:** "The UK all-forces contract — you've supplied every British-pattern sword,
  scabbard and lance since 2008. And in the US, Atlanta Cutlery already supplies government-spec
  Army NCO and Navy sabers. These are yours. The job is to not lose them."
- **Recurring — no public tender:** "The Gurkha and Indian-Army kukri — your oldest line, 1943.
  Runs on relationship, not a bid."
- **Pursue — market & relationship:** "The Gulf presentation-sabre market, and the Make-in-India
  export push."
- **Benchmark:** "And the history — Pooley held the UK ceremonial work to 2018, then stepped back
  against cheap imports. That tells you exactly how Britain decides: cost and continuity."

**Point at the expos:** "Because these deals are won in the room, not on a portal — IDEX Abu Dhabi
in January 2027 and DSEI London that September are where you should physically be."

---

## 3 · Market Expansion — where to point the sales effort

> "If Tender Intelligence is 'what's open', this is 'where should we even be trying'."

**Point at the tiers:** "Three tiers. **Priority** — UK, US, India, Saudi: budget, tradition and
access all line up. **Secondary** — Australia, Canada: strong fit, slower to convert. **Watch** —
Finland, Sweden, Nigeria, South Africa: real tradition but small budgets or hard access."

**Point at a couple of market cards / the budgets:** "These defence budgets are the real,
just-released SIPRI 2025 numbers — the US actually *fell* to 954 billion as Ukraine aid ended,
while India rose to 92 and the UK to 89. Each card gives you the one-paragraph reason to go — or
not go — with that country. Notice we *dropped* Finland and Sweden down: the assumption that
joining NATO meant new sword orders didn't hold up when we checked — they kept their own
ceremonial dress. We'd rather tell you that than sell you a phantom market."

---

## 4 · Competitor Watch — who can actually beat you

> "You asked who your competition is. Here's the honest map."

**Point at the table:** "Only two names carry real contract weight — **Pooley** in Britain, on
the price-and-continuity argument, and **WKC** of Solingen at the premium end. You beat Pooley on
cost and WKC on value. That's the whole tender battlefield."

**Then the rest:** "Everyone else is noise on the contract field. Wilkinson Sword? Famous name,
but they stopped making swords in 2005 — it's a razor brand now. Cold Steel, Hanwei, Albion —
they fight for collectors, not governments. And Marto of Toledo? You *own* it. The value in this
page is that it stops you spending energy on rivals who can't actually take a contract from you."

---

## 5 · Social vs Competitors — the uncomfortable mirror

> "This is the page that usually gets a reaction. It's the one glaring gap."

**Point at the bars:** "On the product, you have no equal. Online, you're being out-shouted.
Real public follower counts: Cold Steel has about 233,000 Instagram followers. Your main brand
handle has about 1,600. That's the 145× gap from the front page — made concrete."

**Point at the influencers:** "And these five people — Shadiversity, Skallagrim, Metatron and
others — have a combined audience in the millions, and they review budget production swords for a
living. That's the category you dominate, and they're barely handling your blades."

**The move:** "The fix is cheap and it's the one thing Cold Steel can never buy: authenticity.
One properly-run channel filming the Dehradun forge making a real British officer's sword, seeded
to those reviewers. Nobody else in the top tier can film a live military contract being made."

## 6 · Brand & Collector Pulse — the heritage asset

> "Beyond governments, you're sitting on something most defence suppliers would kill for."

**Point at the film credits:** "Your blades have been on screen for twenty years. These green
tags are the ones we could confirm against your own materials and licensing records — Gladiator,
Lord of the Rings, Kingdom of Heaven, Star Wars, Harry Potter, Braveheart. The grey ones circulate
widely but we couldn't verify each, so we won't claim them as fact — that discipline is deliberate."

**Point at reputation:** "Collector sentiment is genuinely good — 4.9 out of 5 across 615 reviews
on one aggregator. The recurring niggles — whippy long blades, hilt fit — are a fixable QC-and-
communication story, not a brand problem."

**Point at the 2026 slate:** "And the timing's good — Christopher Nolan's *Odyssey* this July,
new *Game of Thrones* properties, more period drama than in years. Every fresh on-screen credit
refreshes your entire replica line. The move: put the confirmed filmography on one page and pitch
those 2026 armourers directly."

## 7 · Trade & Regulatory Radar — the numbers behind the borders

> "Swords, cutlasses and bayonets all trade under one customs code — 9307. Watching it tells you
> where India's edged-weapon exports flow, and where the tariff maths is about to change."

**Point at the flow bars:** "India's HS-9307 exports are small but growing — the US takes 38% of
them, then Spain, the UK, Germany. Small market, which is exactly why one national dress-sword
contract moves the needle so much."

**Point at the tariff line — the important one:** "Here's a live change you need to know: the US
just put an 18% tariff on India-made blades under the February 2026 trade deal. That directly
raises your landed cost in your biggest market. The play is to finish and assemble through Atlanta
Cutlery on US soil to soften it — and this tool flags it the day it moves."

**One correction we surfaced:** "And a myth-buster — there's no Uttarakhand defence corridor;
India's corridors are UP and Tamil Nadu. We'd rather correct the record than repeat it."

---

## 8 · Geopolitical & Defence Spend — the tailwind

> "This is the weather. Why the whole market is moving your way."

**Point at the total + trend:** "Global defence spending hit 2.89 trillion dollars in 2025 — the
eleventh straight annual rise, the highest share of world GDP since 2009. Bigger budgets mean
bigger ceremonial establishments and more parades — more swords."

**Point at the events:** "And set-piece moments drive spikes — the 2023 Coronation used five state
swords; India's Republic Day is an annual dress-sabre event. We're honest here too: we checked
whether the NATO enlargements created sword demand, and they didn't — those armies kept their own
dress. So the move isn't to chase NATO — it's to aim where the tradition is real: the Commonwealth
and the Gulf."

---

## 9 · Room to Grow — the consumer upside

> "The military business is the core. But you own the factory, the film credits and two collector
> brands — so there's a consumer lane that costs almost nothing to switch on."

**Point at the openings:** "Creator-driven collector sales — that reviewer audience again. A film-
and-TV armoury refresh off the 2026 slate. And HEMA — historical European martial arts — a real,
growing niche; a 2021 census counted over 3,000 practitioners in Germany and Austria alone, and
your blades are the standard entry-level sparring swords."

**The honesty note:** "You'll notice we *don't* put a market-size dollar figure up. The numbers
floating around online are AI-generated estimates with no real methodology, so we won't hand you a
fake one. The move: keep this lane secondary but switched on, and fund it from marketing — never
from the tender budget."

---

## 10 · Signal Feed — everything, in one stream

> "And finally, the wire. Every signal we've talked about — tenders, geopolitics, competitors,
> trade, brand — in one filterable stream."

**Demonstrate the filters:** "Filter by type, or just show me the opportunities, or just the risks.
Every single item ends with an action — not 'here's a fact', but 'here's what to do about it'."

> "In production this is the beating heart — the portals and sources feed it live, and your team
> starts each day here. Today it's populated with real, verified signal so you can see exactly what
> that daily briefing looks like."
