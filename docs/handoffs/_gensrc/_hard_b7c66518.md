# Putin warns oil‑price surge could fuel inflation as Russia pushes a new BRICS‑led trade order
*At the St. Petersburg International Economic Forum, Vladimir Putin linked high oil prices to global inflation, praised China’s growth, and argued that world trade is drifting from WTO rules toward a BRICS‑centric model.*

Russian President Vladimir Putin used the opening plenary of the 29th St. Petersburg International Economic Forum (June 3‑6) to warn that “if oil prices remain on high, it is expensive, it will actually affect the entire chain of economic interaction… it will influence inflation of the leading economies, including inflation in the United States”【SOURCE 1】. The remark, delivered to an audience of business leaders and foreign journalists, underscored Moscow’s view that energy markets remain a lever of macro‑economic stability.  

Putin then turned to a broader structural shift. “I would note the following as an example that the global trade system is ceasing to be the West‑centered. The share of BRICS in global trade in goods has almost doubled over 25 years. The countries of our association accounted for almost a quarter of global exports last year and this figure continues growing steadily,” he said, echoing data in the forum’s briefing material【FACT 1】【QUOTE 1】. He added that “the global trade architecture is gradually moving away from the original principles of the World Trade Organization”【QUOTE 2】, and highlighted that “since the start of the 21st century, the number of bilateral, regional and mega‑regional agreements in the commerce sphere increased by almost four times”【QUOTE 5】.  

The president framed these trends as a strategic opportunity for Russia. “The launch of a new investment cycle is a key task for economic authorities, and investment growth is the most important indicator of efficiency of the work,” he asserted【QUOTE 3】. He linked sovereign capacity to global leadership, noting that “positions in the system of global economic ties and global leadership depend on a particular country’s ability to ensure its own sovereignty”【QUOTE 6】.  

In a separate session with heads of major international news agencies, Putin praised China’s trajectory. “China is developing robustly and playing an increasingly important role in the global economy, world politics and international affairs,” he told the gathering, adding that “the sound relations between the Russian and Chinese heads of state serve as a solid foundation for continuously opening up new prospects for bilateral ties”【SOURCE 2】【QUOTE 4】【QUOTE 7】.  

The forum’s theme, “Pragmatic Dialogue: The Path to a Stable Future,” reflects Moscow’s effort to re‑orient global economic dialogue away from traditional Western‑led institutions toward a multipolar framework anchored by BRICS and strategic partnerships. By coupling a warning on oil‑driven inflation with a narrative of expanding trade ties and investment, Putin signaled that Russia intends to leverage its energy leverage and its alliance with China to shape a new architecture of global commerce.  

If oil prices stay elevated, the Kremlin’s warning suggests that inflationary pressures could ripple through the United States and other advanced economies, potentially prompting policy responses that may further accelerate the shift toward alternative trade blocs. The next steps will likely involve deepening bilateral agreements and courting new investment flows, as Russia seeks to cement its role in a rebalanced world trade order.
